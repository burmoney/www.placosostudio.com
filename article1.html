The Safezones Project

The Safezones Project is a community-driven safety and urban inclusion initiative focused on creating welcoming, secure public environments throughout Guadalajara. Through local partnerships, practical training, and accessible spaces, the project aims to support community well-being and provide help when it is needed.

🌟 Zonas de Respiro Hubs

Zonas de Respiro, or “Respite Zones,” are intended to serve as recognizable places of refuge and support in the city.

  • Immediate sanctuary: Climate-conscious spaces where people—especially women, youth, and marginalized community members—can seek shelter from harassment, extreme heat, or other stressful situations.
  • Emergency connectivity: Communication tools, panic buttons, or direct links to local emergency services can help connect visitors with assistance.
  • Urban renewal: Improving transit areas and public spaces with safer, well-lit, pedestrian-friendly environments.

🛠️ Weekly Skills Workshops

Workshops help residents and volunteers build the skills and confidence to support a safer, more connected community.

  • De-escalation and mental health first aid: Recognize crisis situations and learn safe bystander responses.
  • Practical skills: Build life skills and learn project-planning and emergency procedures.
  • Community sensitization: Help local businesses understand ways to prevent gender-based violence and discrimination.

🤝 Community Partnerships

  • Local businesses: Neighborhood merchants and convenience stores may serve as designated safety drop-in locations.
  • Municipal coordination: Collaboration with organizations such as InMujeresGDL and the Comisaría de Seguridad Ciudadana can support coordinated responses.
  • Transit and mobility: Partnerships with transport networks and community initiatives, including Guardianes de la Ciudad, can help improve safe routes and emergency connections.

🛡️ Inclusive, Easy-to-Recognize Spaces

The project’s approach is based on the idea that safety should be accessible to everyone, including children, people with disabilities, and people of diverse gender identities. Clear signage, consistent colors, and visible lighting can help people identify a Safezone when they need support.
